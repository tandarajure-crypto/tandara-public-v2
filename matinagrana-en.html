THE FOURTH BRANCH OF THE FIRST TANDARA FAMILY

4.0. Mate Tandara — son of Ivan (★ c. 1740 – ✝ N.G.)

Mate Tandara was the fourth son of Ivan Tandara. Mate and his wife Filipa Kopač had five children: a son, Ivan, and daughters Matija, Manda, Jaka and Cecilija. The male line continued exclusively through their son Ivan. Ivan married twice. In his first marriage to Pava Sičenica, he had a son, Ante, the only male heir of Mate's line. In his second marriage to Jela Parlov, he had a daughter, Ruža. This branch is popularly known by the nickname Cesarovići.

An interesting oral tradition concerning the origin of this nickname has been preserved among the living members of this genealogical branch of the Tandara family. According to the story, some of Mate's descendants worked on the land for a family of the Lažeta clan. When members of the household and their neighbours asked them, “What was the food like at the Lažeta household?”, they replied: “Like at the emperor's table!”

Whether the word they used was cesar or sesar remains unclear to this day. Although both forms can be found on older gravestones and in oral tradition, most descendants of this family branch are now known as Cesarovići.

4.1.1.2.2.1.1. Ante — son of Marijan (★ 1961 – ✝ 1991)

As a volunteer, Ante joined the defence of Croatia in the early 1990s against the aggression of the former Yugoslav People's Army and Serbian paramilitary forces.

Ante took part in the Croatian War of Independence from August 1991 as a member of the 105th Brigade of the Croatian Army. In September of that year, Ante and nineteen other Croatian defenders, mostly from Bjelovar, were captured in Kusonje near Pakrac, tortured and killed. Their remains were exhumed from the Rakov Potok mass grave in January 1992.

Ante was married and had two sons, Marijan and Marko.

4.1.1.2.2.1.2. Dinko — son of Marijan (★ 1962 –)

Early Life and Family

Dinko was born in 1962 into the family of Marijan Tandara, whose roots were in Ričice. His father died in Germany in 1963, when Dinko was only one month old, so he never had the opportunity to know him. In 1964, his mother moved with her three children — Ante, Neda and Dinko — to Klokočevac, where they lived in modest and difficult circumstances. Dinko is the youngest of the three children. His brother Ante was later killed as a Croatian defender in Kusonje, while his sister Neda moved to Split.

Education and Sporting Development

Dinko first encountered taekwondo in primary school, in the seventh grade, where he showed exceptional persistence and talent. He later crowned that talent and persistence with a series of titles in the former state. During the 1980s he became one of the most successful competitors:

  • Yugoslav taekwondo champion: 1986–1991 (consecutively)
  • Member of the former state's national taekwondo team
  • Founder and coach of Taekwondo Club Omega in Bjelovar
  • President of Taekwondo Club Omega

Alongside sport, he showed an interest in visual art, attended art workshops and maintained an interest in drawing and graphic art throughout his life.

Croatian War of Independence

At the beginning of the Croatian War of Independence, Dinko and his brother Ante volunteered for the defence of Croatia. He became a member of the Omega Special Police Unit of the Bjelovar-Bilogora Police Administration and served on numerous battlefields throughout Croatia, including Pakrac, Bilogora, Maslenica, Velebit and other areas.

The greatest tragedy of his life occurred on 8 September 1991, on the feast of the Nativity of Mary, when his brother Ante Tandara was killed in Kusonje in the massacre of 20 Croatian defenders. The family searched for his brother for months, living in uncertainty and hope. The first mass grave was discovered in early January 1992, and Ante was among those who had been killed. Dinko took part in the search for the missing, and his testimony about the wartime events forms part of the historical memory of Bjelovar and Croatia.

Professional Career

After the war, Dinko held a number of positions in sport and in the system of care for Croatian veterans:

  • Martial arts instructor
  • Commander of a sports company
  • Head of the kinesiology department
  • Participant in the Neretva Boat Marathon
  • Employee of the Ministry of Croatian Veterans

In public administration, he held two key positions:

  • Assistant Minister of Croatian Veterans
  • Director of the Directorate for Advisory, Psychosocial and Health Assistance at the Ministry of Croatian Veterans

Dinko Tandara has never been a member of any political party. He often says that he recognises only one coalition — a coalition with people. Because of this attitude and his way of working, he is respected by veterans, athletes, politicians and ordinary citizens.

Retirement and Creative Work

He retired after many years of work within the system of care for Croatian veterans. He devoted himself to his family and to his many hobbies. He equipped a woodworking workshop in which he makes various objects from wood and other materials, ranging from practical pieces of furniture to original handicrafts created according to his own ideas and solutions. His ingenuity, patience and inclination toward practical creativity are particularly evident in this work.

He also works on the family property and in agriculture, drives a tractor on his mother's property in Klokočevac, and has patented some of his handmade creations. On one occasion he converted an ordinary car into a convertible, further illustrating his creativity, technical skill and desire to turn his own ideas into concrete and practical solutions.

Family

Dinko and his wife Dubravka née Lončar have two sons, Luka and Pavle. He has a granddaughter who trains in taekwondo and a grandson. His sister Neda lives in Split, his mother in Klokočevac, and his nephews — the sons of his late brother Ante — live in Bjelovar. Family ties and togetherness have remained a lasting foundation of his life.

Summary

Dinko was a Croatian defender, athlete, educator, creator and humanitarian. His life journey encompasses personal tragedy, wartime courage, sporting achievements, professional dedication and creative energy that continues to motivate him today. In all these roles, he has remained what people value most — a human being.
