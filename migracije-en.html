Migration and Distribution of the Tandara Family

Introduction

This chapter follows the movement of individual branches of the Tandara family after they emerged in their ancestral homeland. Rather than repeating the history of how the surname originated, it focuses on migration routes, the periods during which those movements can be traced, and the places where new family centres were established.

Ričice remains the starting point of this account, while every subsequent location is viewed as part of a migration route that must be connected with a specific family branch and the available sources.

Ričice — the Family's Place of Origin

The earliest reliably known centre of the family is Ričice in the Imotski region. It is here, during the first half of the 18th century, that the development of the family from which the later branches descended can be traced.

Over the centuries, Ričice was more than simply a place of residence. It represents the point of origin from which individual family members gradually moved to surrounding areas while retaining an awareness of their shared ancestry.

Expansion Within the Imotski Region

During the 18th and 19th centuries, the number of family members gradually increased. As new households were established, some families moved to nearby places in search of arable land, the opportunity to establish homes of their own, or better economic conditions.

Such migrations were common among nearly all families in the Imotski region and represented a natural continuation of demographic growth.

Expansion Towards Zavelim, Tomislavgrad, and Livno

The first reliably documented spread of the Tandara surname beyond Ričice is associated with the Zavelim area, a broad region comprising about ten villages. Among them is the village of Tandara, named after the first settlers from Ričice — members of the Tandara family.

Between 1806 and 1819, construction of the first houses in that area began, followed by the gradual relocation of two families from Ričice. According to family tradition and research conducted to date, these were the family of Ante Antin, founder of Lovro's branch and later Križan's branch, and the family of Martin Petrov, founder of Toma's branch and the Tromići line. Their arrival established the Zavelim branch of the Tandara family as a direct continuation of the original Ričice lineage.

The Zavelim branch played a key role in preserving the surname, family identity, and lasting connection with the family's roots in Ričice. The settlement of these families created a new family centre from which additional lines later developed.

The second expansion of the Tandara family most likely led towards the Tomislavgrad area. From there, some families moved to Livno, where their descendants still live today. The Tandara families of Livno will be covered in a separate chapter entitled The Tandara Family of Livno.

Emigration to Europe

The second half of the 20th century brought a new wave of migration. Economic circumstances led numerous families to leave for temporary or permanent employment in Western European countries.

Most emigrants settled in:

  • Germany
  • Austria
  • Switzerland
  • the United Kingdom

Although they established new homes abroad, most maintained a strong connection with their homeland, regularly visiting Ričice and preserving family ties.

Overseas Migration

During the 20th century, some members of the family also emigrated overseas. Their most common destinations were:

  • the United States of America
  • Canada
  • Australia

In their new communities, the families continued to foster their Croatian identity, customs, and awareness of their origins.

Distribution of the Tandara Family Over Time

The story of the Tandara family in Ričice begins very modestly. When the first known Tandara, or Tandarić, family was recorded in Netrub in 1725, it consisted of just three members. Over the next three centuries, that small family nucleus grew into a family whose descendants gradually spread from Ričice to other parts of Croatia, neighbouring regions, and ultimately countries around the world.

By 1806, Ričice was home to three Tandara families with a total of 20 members. About fifteen years later, four families with 27 members were recorded in Ričice and Zavelim. The growth in the number of descendants was accompanied by gradual expansion to new places, most often in search of land, work, and better living conditions.

The records from 1948 provide a particularly valuable snapshot. At that time, 21 Tandara families with 107 members were recorded in Croatia across nine locations. Most still lived in Ričice — 12 families and 69 people — while other families had already settled in Zagreb, Split, Kutjevo, Podravska Slatina, the areas around Bjelovar and Đakovo, and elsewhere.

According to the project's data for 2026, Croatia alone is home to more than 105 Tandara families with over 330 members. At the same time, it is estimated that approximately as many members of the Tandara family now live outside Croatia as within it. This estimate includes descendants and family branches dispersed across several countries and should be regarded as a working estimate, as equally complete and comparable official data are not available for every country.

The numbers shown beside the markers are not an official census, but rather a working overview derived from the genealogical data collected to date. Where the data do not allow the number of families or members to be determined reliably, the marker explicitly says so instead of giving an estimated figure.

Romanian and Slovak families bearing the Tandara surname are not included on this map, because research to date has not confirmed shared roots or a genealogical connection with the Croatian Tandara family. Likewise, Australian place names, hotels, and other uses of the name “Tandara” are not included; the Australian marker refers exclusively to Australia as a recorded destination of emigrants from the Croatian family.

These figures tell more than a story of demographic growth. They also trace one family's migration journey over three centuries: from a single three-member family in Netrub, through gradual expansion within Croatia, to present-day generations living in different countries and on different continents. Each new location on the map represents a new family story, a new branch, and a continuation of their shared roots.

From three members of one family in 1725 to hundreds of descendants three centuries later — that continuity and geographic spread best demonstrate how migration shaped the Tandara family as it exists today.

How Migration Routes Are Verified

The mere appearance of the same surname in a new place is not enough to prove that a person belongs to a particular branch. More reliable connections are established by examining family relationships, the sequence of generations, places of birth and marriage, family tradition, and other available records.

For this reason, both the map and the text distinguish documented migration routes from routes that remain research hypotheses.

Present-Day Connections Between Dispersed Branches

Modern communications allow descendants of distant branches to reconnect, exchange photographs and documents, and compare family information. Such contacts can help fill gaps in older archival sources that cannot be resolved using records from a single place.

Conclusion

The migrations of the Tandara family can be viewed as a series of connected routes beginning in Ričice and leading towards neighbouring areas, other parts of Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Western Europe, and overseas countries.

This page therefore does not serve as a second genealogy, but as a geographic layer of family history: it shows where individual branches moved and which connections still require confirmation through additional documents.
