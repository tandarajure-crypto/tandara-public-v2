Migration and Distribution of the Tandara Family

Introduction

This chapter follows the movement of individual branches of the Tandara family after they had become established in their original area. Rather than repeating the history of the surname's origin, the emphasis is on migration routes, the periods in which these movements can be traced, and the places where new family centres developed.

Ričice remains the starting point of this presentation, while every later location is viewed as part of a migration path that should be connected with a specific family branch and the available sources.

Ričice – the Family's Place of Origin

The earliest reliably known centre of the family is Ričice in the Imotski region. It is here that, during the first half of the 18th century, the development of the family from which the later branches emerged can be followed.

For centuries, Ričice was more than simply a place of residence. It represents the point of origin from which individual family members gradually moved towards neighbouring areas while maintaining an awareness of their common ancestry.

Expansion Within the Imotski Region

During the 18th and 19th centuries, the number of family members gradually increased. As new households were formed, some families moved to nearby places in search of cultivable land, an opportunity to establish their own homes, or better economic conditions.

Such migrations were common among almost all families of the Imotski region and represented a natural continuation of demographic development.

Expansion Towards Zavelim, Tomislavgrad and Livno

The first reliably documented expansion of the Tandara surname beyond Ričice is connected with the Zavelim area, a broad region containing around ten villages. Among them is the village of Tandara, named after the first settlers from Ričice — members of the Tandara family.

Between 1806 and 1819, construction of the first houses in this area began, followed by the gradual relocation of two families from Ričice. According to family tradition and research carried out so far, these were the family of Ante Antin, ancestor of the Lovro branch and later the Križan branch, and the family of Martin Petrov, ancestor of the Toma branch and the Tromić line. Their arrival marked the formation of the Zavelim branch of the Tandara family, a direct continuation of the original Ričice line.

The Zavelim branch played a key role in preserving the surname, family identity and lasting connection with the roots in Ričice. The arrival of these families created a new family centre from which additional family lines later developed.

A second expansion of the Tandara family most likely led towards the Tomislavgrad area. From there, some families moved to Livno, where their descendants still live today. The Tandara families of Livno will be presented in a separate chapter entitled The Livno Tandaras.

Emigration to Europe

The second half of the 20th century brought a new wave of migration. Economic circumstances led many families to leave temporarily or permanently for work in Western European countries.

The largest numbers settled in:

  • Germany
  • Austria
  • Switzerland
  • the United Kingdom

Although they established new homes there, most retained strong ties with their place of origin, regularly visiting Ričice and maintaining family connections.

Overseas Migration

During the 20th century, some members of the family also emigrated overseas. The most common destinations were:

  • the United States of America
  • Canada
  • Australia

In their new surroundings, families continued to preserve their Croatian identity, customs and awareness of their origins.

Distribution of the Tandara Family Through Time

The story of the Tandara family in Ričice begins very modestly. When the first known Tandara, or Tandarić, family was recorded in Netrub in 1725, it consisted of only three members. From this small family nucleus, over the following three centuries, developed a family whose descendants gradually spread from Ričice to other parts of Croatia, neighbouring regions and eventually to countries throughout the world.

By 1806, Ričice was home to three Tandara families with 20 members, and around fifteen years later Ričice and Zavelim together had four families with 27 members. The growth in the number of descendants was accompanied by gradual expansion to new places, most often in search of land, employment and better living conditions.

A particularly valuable snapshot is provided by the situation in 1948. At that time, Croatia had 21 Tandara families with 107 members, distributed across nine locations. Most still lived in Ričice — 12 families and 69 people — while other families were already living in Zagreb, Split, Kutjevo, Podravska Slatina, the Bjelovar area, Đakovo and elsewhere.

According to the information currently available to the project for 2026, Croatia alone is home to more than 105 Tandara families with more than 330 members. At the same time, it is estimated that approximately the same number of Tandaras live outside Croatia as within Croatia. This estimate includes descendants and family branches distributed across several countries and should be regarded as a working estimate because equally complete and comparable official data are not available for every country.

The map shows only the locations and countries listed in the currently available genealogical HTML data for the Croatian Tandara family. Where a city or village is known, the marker is placed at that location. Where only the country is known, the marker is placed approximately at its geographical centre.

The figures accompanying the markers are not an official population census, but a working record derived from the genealogical information currently collected. Where the available information does not allow the number of families or members to be established reliably, this is stated explicitly in the marker instead of estimating a number.

Romanian and Slovak Tandaras are not included on this map, because research carried out so far has not confirmed a common origin or genealogical relationship with the Croatian Tandara family. Likewise, Australian place-names, hotels and other entities named “Tandara” are not part of this map; the Australian marker refers exclusively to Australia as a recorded destination of emigration by members of the Croatian Tandara family.

These figures do not speak only about demographic growth. They also show the migration journey of one family across three centuries: from one three-member family in Netrub, through gradual expansion within Croatia, to present-day generations living in different countries and on different continents. Every new location on this map represents a new family story, a new branch and a continuation of the same common root.

From three members of a single family in 1725 to several hundred descendants three centuries later — this continuity and distribution show more clearly than anything else how migration shaped the Tandara family as it exists today.

How Migration Routes Are Verified

The mere appearance of the same surname in a new place is not sufficient to prove that a person belongs to a particular family branch. More reliable connections are established by examining family relationships, the sequence of generations, places of birth and marriage, family tradition and other available records.

For this reason, both the map and the text distinguish documented migration routes from directions that still remain research hypotheses.

Present-Day Connections Between Dispersed Branches

Modern communication makes it possible for descendants of distant branches to reconnect, exchange photographs and documents, and compare family information. Such contacts can help fill gaps that cannot be resolved from older archival sources in only one location.

Conclusion

The migrations of the Tandara family can be viewed as a series of connected routes beginning in Ričice and leading towards neighbouring areas, other parts of Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Western Europe and overseas countries.

This page therefore does not serve as a second genealogy, but as the geographical layer of family history: it shows where individual branches moved and which connections still require confirmation through additional documentation.
